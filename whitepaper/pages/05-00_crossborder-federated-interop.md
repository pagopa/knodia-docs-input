This chapter reports the features that the actors involved in the *Cross-Border Federation*, primarily the *Interop Nodes*, must ensure in order to enable interoperability between multiple independent Interoperability Domains.

The *Cross-Border Federation* extends the Italian interoperability model based on the PDND, introduced in the previous chapter and adopted in Italy to enable interaction between the IT systems of public administrations and between these and the IT systems of recognized private subjects.

The *Cross-Border Federation* is meant to support a case like that of the European Union. Each Interoperability Domain – corresponding to a Member State or, in general, a set of organizations identified with certainty (such as the agencies of the European Commission) – maintains its autonomy regarding interoperability within its perimeter of competence. The objective here is to allow digital interaction between the IT systems of the organizations of these Interoperability Domains through shared mechanisms based on mutual inter-domain trust. These interactions are hereinafter referred to as cross-border interactions.

The *Cross-Border Federation* is designed in compliance with EIF Recommendation 26. Concerning interoperability agreements between organizations in the provision of a European public service, the Recommentation requires to "establish interoperability agreements in all layers, complemented by operational agreements". Specifically, within the *Cross-Border Federation*, the cited interoperability agreements are digitally materialized through authentication of the involved organization and execution of the actions required to verify the regulatory prerequisites for the sharing of data and services between them. The related operational agreements are implemented by defining and sharing the mechanisms implemented by the IT systems of the organizations interested in cross-border interactions.

The *Cross-Border Federation* considers the interoperability levels described by the EIF itself, specifically:

- legal interoperability: ensures compliance with the European regulatory framework on interoperability and on the protection of personal data of natural persons, preserving the possibility of integrations by Member States;
- organizational interoperability: specifies the relationships between the organizations involved and the processes they follow to enable cross-border interactions between them;
- semantic interoperability: identifies the common semantic objects (controlled vocabularies, ontologies, and data schemas) necessary to enable cross-border interactions;
- tchnical interoperability: defines a shared federated protocol that interested organizations implement to enable cross-border interactions between their IT systems.
